Step 1: Intake & Product Definition The success of the data certification process begins with a thorough intake and asset definition. This foundational step ensures that all relevant aspects are considered before any certification activities commence. By clearly defining the parameters and requirements of the data asset, organizations can set the stage for successful outcomes and effective delivery. Required Elements Product Name, Description, and Domain: Provide a clear name for the data product, along with a concise description that summarizes its purpose and relevant domain. This helps ensure clarity and avoids ambiguity among users. Insight: “Standardize naming conventions and descriptions across the organization to enhance understanding and consistency.” Business Purpose and Supported Process: Clearly articulate the business purpose of the data asset and the specific processes it supports. This helps stakeholders understand how the asset contributes to business objectives and decision-making. Insight: “Engage with key stakeholders to gather insights on the business needs and ensure the product aligns with strategic goals. This can be done through workshops or interviews to capture expectations.” Intended Use Cases and Consumer Personas: Identify the intended use cases for the data asset and document the profiles of consumer personas who will utilize it. This information clarifies the context in which the data will be used and enhances understanding of user requirements. Insight: “Conduct user research to identify various consumer personas and their needs for utilizing the data. User stories can also be helpful in communicating how different personas will interact with the data asset.” Source Systems and Key Data Elements: Document the source systems from which the data product derives its information and outline the key data elements involved. Understanding the origins of the data is crucial for assessing data quality and lineage. Insight: “Create data flow diagrams to visually represent the source systems and data pathways. This can aid in understanding how data is transformed and integrated.” Critical Data Elements (CDEs): Identify and define Critical Data Elements that are vital for the product's functionality and compliance. CDEs require higher levels of integrity and oversight, given their significance to business operations. Insight: “Collaborate with business and data management teams to classify CDEs based on their impact on decision-making and regulatory obligations.” Ownership and Stewardship: Establish clear ownership and stewardship roles for the data asset. Knowing who is responsible for managing and maintaining the asset is key to ensuring accountability. Insight: “Designate a Business Data Steward and a Data Steward for each asset to provide continuous oversight and engagement in the certification process.” Data Sensitivity and Criticality Tier: Evaluate the sensitivity of the product and assign a criticality tier that reflects the level of risk associated with its use. This allows organizations to implement the appropriate governance and security measures. Insight: “Conduct a risk assessment to determine sensitivity and criticality levels, involving relevant stakeholders from compliance and security teams.” Schedule and Reporting Timeline, Period: Define a clear schedule for the certification process, along with timelines for reporting on progress and outcomes. This helps manage expectations and ensures timely completion of the certification activities. Insight: “Utilize project management tools to create a timeline that aligns with business priorities, integrating checkpoints for progress monitoring.” Data Steward may utilize the template provided in Appendix 1 as a foundational tool to structure your inputs, ensuring that all the critical elements are comprehensively addressed. By customizing and updating this template to reflect the specific needs of your data product, you can streamline the intake and asset definition process, making it more efficient and focused. This approach not only accelerates the certification process but also ensures that all necessary details are captured accurately, supporting a thorough understanding and alignment with business objectives. By diligently addressing these required elements during the intake and asset definition, organizations establish a solid foundation for the certification process. This preparatory work enhances the likelihood of achieving successful outcomes, delivering reliable and high-quality data products that meet business needs and compliance requirements. Engaging with stakeholders, utilizing best practices, and employing effective tools will facilitate a well-thought-out approach to certification.
