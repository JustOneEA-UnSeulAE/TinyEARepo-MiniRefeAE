Step 5: Certification Decision & Publication ()
Step 5: Certification Decision & Publication The fifth step in the data certification process centers on the decision-making regarding the certification of data assets and the subsequent publication of those decisions. This step is crucial for establishing the status of data assets and ensuring that stakeholders are informed about the quality and usage conditions associated with them. Decision Inputs In making a certification decision, several key inputs must be considered. Data Quality (DQ) Results and Residual Risks: The outcomes of previous data quality assessments are essential inputs for decision-making. This includes an evaluation of any residual risks associated with the product and underlying data assets, which must be understood to determine whether the product meets the certification criteria. A comprehensive analysis of data quality metrics will provide insights into the reliability and usability of the product. Lineage Completeness: Verification of the completeness of data lineage is crucial for establishing trust in the data. Ensuring that the lineage is fully documented - covering the flow of data from source to end-use - is a significant factor in the certification process. Incomplete lineage verification or established lineage may raise concerns over data integrity and the accuracy of reporting. Compliance Validation: An assessment of compliance with relevant regulations and internal policies must be included in the decision-making process. This validation ensures that the data asset adheres to legal requirements, data governance standards, and any applicable privacy laws. Compliance is non-negotiable to maintain the organization’s reputation and avoid legal ramifications. Outcomes Based on the evaluation of the above inputs, the certification decision will fall into one of the following categories: Approved: The data asset has met all criteria for certification and is deemed fit for use. Users can rely on its integrity, quality, and compliance with applicable standards. Conditionally Approved: The data asset meets some certification criteria but has certain conditions that must be addressed before it can be fully certified. These conditions should be documented, and a plan for resolving them should be established. Rejected: The data asset does not meet the necessary certification standards and is not approved for use. This outcome necessitates a review of the asset to identify areas for improvement and potential pathways to re-certification. Certification Publication Once a decision has been made, the certification must be published to ensure that stakeholders are informed of the asset’s status. The publication process includes: Data Catalog Badge: An official badge or mark indicating the certification status of the data asset is added to the data catalog. This visual representation helps users quickly identify certified assets. Validity Period: The certification publication should include the duration for which the certification is valid. This informs users about the timeframe within which the certification is guaranteed, emphasizing the need for regular re-evaluation. Known Limitations: Any limitations associated with the data asset should be clearly communicated in the publication. This includes notes on potential issues, constraints on usage, or specific conditions that users must consider. Transparency around limitations promotes informed decision-making among users. Certification Decision & Publication is a vital phase in the data certification lifecycle, ensuring that all decisions are based on comprehensive, objective assessments. By carefully evaluating inputs such as data quality results, lineage completeness, and compliance validation, organizations can make informed certification decisions that enhance data integrity and governance. The publication of certification statuses fosters trust and clarity among stakeholders, ultimately supporting effective data utilization across the organization.
