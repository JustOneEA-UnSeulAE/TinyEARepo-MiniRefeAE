SaaS Agreements: Key Contractual Provisions

Henry Ward Classen | Nov 15, 2021 34 min read
Cybersecurity Data Privacy Internet Law & Cybersecurity

Summary
Under the SaaS (“Software as a Service”) model, a cloud provider hosts or provides access to a software application, allowing customers to access it as a service on an as-needed basis instead of licensing a copy of software.
The SaaS model allows cloud providers to reduce costs and improve service and allows customers to reduce costs, save time, and receive up-to-date improvements. However, customers relinquish control over their IT to a third party and must agree to the cloud providers’ conditions.
Contractual provisions should specify information about services, pricing and payment, service level agreements, disaster recovery, indemnification, limitation of liability, term of the agreement, suspension and termination, transition rights, compliance obligations, acceptable use policies, and data issues.
Data issues that should be covered in the contract include ownership of data; cloud provider access to and use of customer data; data retention; and data security, which encompasses security standards, location of data, physical security, technical security, administrative security, and security breaches and incidents.
