DATA CERTIFICATION LIFECYCLE ()
7. Data Certification Lifecycle Overview The data certification lifecycle is a structured process designed to ensure that data assets meet quality, governance, and compliance standards. This lifecycle consists of several key steps, each critical to delivering certified data assets that align with business objectives and user needs. Intake & Scoping: This initial step involves receiving requests for certification from business units, Certification Workgroup (Data Trustee or Governance Committee), or other departments(Senior executives asking for reports). These requests may pertain to reports, data products, or specific data assets that require official certification. During this stage, the scope of the certification effort is defined, identifying the purpose and importance of the data being assessed. Lineage Discovery & Profiling: In this step, the designated Data Steward and Data Custodian conduct a thorough discovery of data lineage, documenting the flow of data from its source to its final destination. This involves analyzing the connections and transformations that data undergoes through various systems. A key question to address in this phase is whether the raw data needed for collection and analysis is accessible and available to contribute to the final product. Additionally, data profiling is performed to assess the characteristics of the data, ensuring its availability and identifying any potential quality issues. Quality & Lineage Validation: This step focuses on defining data quality metrics and validating the lineage established in the earlier stages. Data quality is assessed along multiple dimensions, including accuracy, completeness, timeliness, validity, consistency, and uniqueness. The lineage validation confirms that all transformations are documented and that there is a clear understanding of how data moves through systems. Governance, Compliance & Change Readiness: This step ensures that all data assets align with governance frameworks and comply with relevant regulations. Governance procedures involve documentation of ownership and alignment with standards such as DAMA-DMBOK. Compliance assessments address privacy considerations (e.g., PIPEDA/GDPR) and verify that appropriate access controls and retention policies are in place. Additionally, readiness for changes is evaluated, including defining a change process and agreeing upon conditions for re-certification. Certification Decision & Publication: Following validation and compliance checks, a certification decision is made based on inputs such as data quality results, residual risks, and lineage completeness. Possible outcomes include "Approved," "Conditionally Approved," or "Rejected." When approved, the certification is published with details such as a catalog badge, validity period, and known limitations to inform users about the asset's status(Purview). Ongoing Monitoring & Re-Certification: The final step of the lifecycle involves ongoing monitoring of certified data assets to ensure they maintain quality and compliance over time. This includes tracking quality trend metrics, detecting changes in lineage, and monitoring adherence to service level agreements (SLAs). Re-certification may be triggered by significant changes, quality degradation, or the expiration of the initial certification period. The Data Certification Lifecycle Overview provides a comprehensive framework for assessing and certifying data assets. Each step plays a crucial role in establishing trust in data quality and governance, ensuring that organizations can leverage their data assets effectively for decision-making and operational success.
