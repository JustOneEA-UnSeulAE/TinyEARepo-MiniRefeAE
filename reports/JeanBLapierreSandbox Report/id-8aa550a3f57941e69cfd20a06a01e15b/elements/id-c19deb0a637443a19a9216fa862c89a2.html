Step 4: Governance, Compliance & Change Readiness The Governance, Compliance, and Change Readiness step is a vital component of the data certification process. By establishing a robust governance framework and ensuring compliance with relevant regulations, organizations can mitigate risks associated with data management. Additionally, preparing for potential changes ensures that data assets remain relevant and valuable throughout their lifecycle. Governance Alignment with standards and best practices (DAMA-DMBOK), and Internal Policies: Ensure that the governance practices adhere to industry standards, such as the DAMA-DMBOK framework, alongside any internal policies that dictate data management practices. This alignment guarantees that the organization follows established best practices in data stewardship and governance. Ownership Documented in Data Catalog: Clear documentation of data ownership is essential for accountability and transparency. By maintaining an up-to-date data catalog that specifies each data asset's owner, organizations can ensure that responsibilities are clearly defined and that data stewards are actively engaged in managing their respective assets. To ensure transparency regarding the responsibilities and steps involved in Data Certification work by various stakeholders, the RACI matrix provided in Appendix 6 (RACI Matrix) will serve as a guiding tool for streamlining accountabilities and establishing communication channels. Compliance Privacy Classification (PIPEDA/GDPR): Conduct a thorough evaluation of data assets to ensure compliance with relevant privacy regulations, such as PIPEDA (Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act) or GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation). Proper classification of data assets helps address privacy concerns and protects sensitive information. Access Controls Validated: Implement and validate access controls to safeguard data against unauthorized access. This involves checking that only authorized personnel can access sensitive data assets, ensuring that measures are in place to protect data integrity and confidentiality. Retention and Disposal Rules Applied: Establish clear retention and disposal policies for data assets to comply with legal and regulatory requirements. Verify that these rules are consistently applied to prevent the unintended retention of unnecessary data, thereby reducing risk and storage costs. Change Enablement Change Process Model Defined: Develop a change management process model that outlines how changes to data assets will be handled. This model should include the steps for assessing, implementing, and documenting changes, ensuring that all updates follow a structured approach that minimizes disruptions. Re-certification Triggers Agreed: Define triggers for re-certification that specify the circumstances under which data assets must undergo a new certification process. This could be due to significant changes in data sources, transformations, or regulatory requirements. By having agreed-upon triggers, organizations can maintain data asset integrity and compliance over time. Governance, Compliance & Change Readiness provides essential guidelines for ensuring that data assets are managed and governed effectively. By aligning with established frameworks, documenting ownership, and adhering to compliance regulations, organizations can mitigate risks related to data breaches and enhance the reliability of their data assets. Implementing change enablement practices prepares organizations for future adaptations, ensuring that data remains accurate, compliant, and valuable. To assist in executing this step, best practice guidelines and templates (Appendix 5 – Data Governance & Compliance Checklist)can aid in the review process, ensuring that all necessary governance and compliance measures are comprehensively considered and addressed.
