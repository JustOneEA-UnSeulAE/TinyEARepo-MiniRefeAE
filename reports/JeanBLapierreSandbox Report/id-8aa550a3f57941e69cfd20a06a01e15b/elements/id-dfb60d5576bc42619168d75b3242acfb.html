Step 6: Ongoing Monitoring & Re-Certification The Ongoing Monitoring and Re-Certification step is critical to maintaining the integrity, reliability, and compliance of data assets throughout their lifecycle. Continuous monitoring ensures that data remains fit for purpose, while a structured re-certification process guarantees that any changes or concerns are addressed promptly. Monitoring Effective monitoring involves several key activities designed to uphold the quality and usability of certified data assets: Quality Trend Metrics: Regularly assessing quality trend metrics allows organizations to track the ongoing performance of data assets over time. This includes monitoring key indicators such as accuracy, completeness, and timeliness, enabling data stewards to identify potential issues before they impact business processes. Lineage Change Detection: Monitoring for changes in data lineage is essential for ensuring that any modifications to the data flow are documented and understood. This includes detecting alterations in source systems, changes in data transformations, or updates to output reports. Effective lineage change detection helps maintain transparency and trust in the data. SLA Monitoring: Service Level Agreement (SLA) monitoring ensures that data assets meet predefined performance and quality standards. Regular reviews of SLA adherence are crucial for monitoring data availability, accessibility, and quality, and for ensuring that the organization is meeting its data governance commitments. Re-Certification Re-certification is a proactive measure designed to ensure that data assets remain compliant and continue to meet quality standards. It is triggered under several circumstances: Material Change: Any significant change to the data asset - such as modifications in the source system, changes in the transformation processes, or new regulatory requirements - will necessitate re-certification. Material changes can affect data quality and integrity, making it essential to re-evaluate the asset against certification criteria. Quality Degradation: If monitoring activities indicate a decline in data quality - e.g., an increase in errors, missing records, or failure to meet established quality metrics - the asset should undergo re-certification. Addressing quality degradation promptly is crucial for maintaining trust in the data and ensuring that it continues to serve its intended purpose effectively. Expiry: Certifications are not permanent; they have an established validity period. As the expiration date approaches, a scheduled re-certification process should be initiated to assess the data asset afresh. This ensures that all relevant changes and quality assessments are taken into account before extending the certification status. Ongoing Monitoring & Re-Certification is vital for sustaining the value and integrity of certified data assets over time. By implementing robust monitoring practices that track quality trends, lineage changes, and SLA compliance, organizations can proactively manage data quality and mitigate risks. Additionally, a structured re-certification process ensures that data assets continue to meet evolving business needs and compliance requirements, ultimately reinforcing trust in organizational data.
