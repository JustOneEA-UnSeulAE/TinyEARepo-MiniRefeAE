3. Scope & Applicability In Scope • Enterprise and domain-level data products: Certification is applicable to data assets and data products that are utilized across the enterprise and specific domains within the organization. This includes datasets that inform strategic decision-making, operational processes, and cross-functional initiatives. By certifying these data products, organizations can ensure that the data aligns with business objectives and meets the requisite quality and governance standards necessary for reliable use. • Certified analytical, reporting, and regulatory datasets: Any datasets specifically developed for analytical purposes, reporting, or compliance with regulatory requirements are included in the certification scope. These datasets must demonstrate accuracy, completeness, and timeliness, providing confidence for users who rely on them for informed decision-making, reporting obligations, and regulatory adherence. Certifying these datasets helps organizations mitigate risks associated with data-driven insights and ensures accountability in reporting practices. Out of Scope • Exploratory, Experimental or temporary datasets: Data that is primarily for personal use or exploratory analysis does not fall within the certification process. Temporary datasets, which may be created for short-term projects or one-time analyses, are also excluded. These types of data typically do not require the same level of governance or oversight, as they may not be utilized beyond their immediate purpose. By excluding these datasets from certification, organizations can focus resources on the data that is critical to operations and key decision-making. • Ad-hoc analysis not shared broadly: Any analysis that is conducted on an ad-hoc basis and not intended for broader distribution or operational use are considered out of scope for certification. This includes analysis that does not form part of standardized reporting or approved processes. Such analyses may lack the consistency and rigor required for certification, thus falling outside established data governance frameworks.
