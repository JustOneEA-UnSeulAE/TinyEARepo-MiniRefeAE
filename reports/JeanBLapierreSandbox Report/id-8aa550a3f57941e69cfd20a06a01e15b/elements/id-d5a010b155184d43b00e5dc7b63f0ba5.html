PILLAR 1: Putting Canada on a path to end homelessness in 10 years ()

Canada has the resources, the people, and the will to end homelessness—but every level of government and each sector has a role to play. It requires forward-thinking action: stable housing and a comprehensive set of services—including health care, mental health and addictions treatment, crisis services, and other community supports. We are committed to changing how Canada addresses homelessness and supports those who are unhoused, because every Canadian should have the security of a warm home. That is why Budget 2026 is putting significant federal resources toward helping create the conditions to end homelessness within 10 years. This is a call to action for every level of government, Indigenous partners, the private sector, and the not-for-profit sector to come together to reach this goal.

Homelessness remains one of Canada's most urgent social challenges. But homelessness is not inevitable. It is the result of gaps across housing, health, mental health, addictions, and social support systems. Addressing it requires coordinated action from all levels of government, Indigenous partners, service providers, communities, and the private and public sectors. Budget 2026 proposes a renewed partnership: one in which the federal government invests capital in housing and infrastructure, while provinces and territories will take the lead in ongoing operations and wraparound services, like health care and mental health and substance abuse supports.

Budget 2026 sets Canada on a path to end homelessness over the next decade through strong measures to provide help, housing, and health to Canadians experiencing homelessness.

Help: We will work closely with provinces, municipalities, and philanthropy to provide the help people need as they transition from experiencing homelessness and find a permanent home. This includes jobs, training, and income supports.
Housing: As part of this plan, the Government of Canada is making a historic, multi-billion-dollar investment to build the transitional and permanent housing necessary to pragmatically ending the homelessness crisis.
Health: For those who need it, we will build the facilities needed to care for and support the most vulnerable in our society.
To deliver on this new partnership and reach the goal of ending homelessness within 10 years, Budget 2026 proposes to provide $11.2 billion, over 4 years, starting in 2027-28, and $17.8 billion in future years. These investments will:

Help people move from homelessness into stable housing:
Budget 2026 proposes to provide $1.8 billion over four years, starting in 2027-28, and $3.2 billion in future years to establish a new Integrated Supports Fund, delivered through Build Canada Homes. The Fund would support projects that bring housing together with health, mental health, addictions, and social supports. The Fund would also help develop new transitional and supportive housing, adapt existing buildings, and mobilise public, private, and philanthropic capital behind an integrated housing and care model.
Budget 2026 also proposes to provide an additional $3.6 billion over four years, starting in 2027-28, and $6.4 billion in future years, through Build Canada Homes to build new supportive and transitional housing across Canada. Federal funding will support capital costs, while provincial and territorial partners will be expected to fund ongoing operations and wraparound services, like job, training, income and health supports. This approach would help ensure that new housing is matched with the care and supports people need to remain housed.
Build community mental health and addictions facilities: Budget 2026 proposes to provide $2 billion over two years, starting in 2027-28, for the Health Infrastructure Fund. Facilities funded will provide care before people reach a crisis point, helping address the mental health and substance use challenges that are contributing to homelessness, instability, and growing pressures on communities across Canada.
Maintain support for existing programs to help prevent homelessness: Budget 2026 proposes to provide $2.1 billion over three years, beginning in 2028-29, and $4.4 billion in future years to Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada to renew the Reaching Home initiative and the Veterans Homelessness Program for an additional 10 years. This funding will maintain community-led prevention and homelessness services, including dedicated support for veterans experiencing homelessness. To maximise outcomes, Reaching Home will require rigorous reporting, including real-time data, as a condition for continued funding. More details on the enhanced initiative will be released early in the new year.
Recognising the critical role of provinces and territories and sustaining partnerships to support low-income housing:

Budget 2026 proposes to renew the Canada Housing Benefit for 10 years ($1.7 billion over 3 years, starting in 2028-29, and $3.9 billion in future years), providing direct monthly rental assistance to low-income Canadians, helping prevent homelessness and enabling vulnerable individuals and families to secure and maintain stable housing.
The government will work with the provinces and territories on other elements of their bilateral agreements, ahead of the Spring Economic Update 2027. This work will align these agreements with the goal of putting Canada on a path to end homelessness in 10 years, protect existing non-market housing stock, take into consideration the expanded role of Build Canada Homes, and make responsibilities clearer for delivering affordable housing and targeted affordability supports. As we increase capital investments, provinces and territories will be expected to strengthen funding for operations, health care, and wraparound supports, and to demonstrate progress against shared outcomes.
