Reducing CPP contribution rate The spring economic statement also aims to keep more money in Canadians’ wallets by reducing the amount they have to contribute to the Canada Pension Plan. Starting in January, the CPP contribution rate will be reduced from 9.9 per cent to 9.5 per cent. According to O’Riordan, the change likely indicates the CPP fund is actuarily sound and performing well, allowing the federal government to pass along some of the surplus from it to Canadians. “This change would maintain a prudent financial buffer to protect the CPP against future economic and demographic risks, while providing meaningful contribution relief,” the spring economic update reads. “A 40-basis point reduction in the CPP contribution would translate into annual savings of about $133 for an employee earning $70,000 a year, with equivalent savings for their employer,” it also projects.
