BDM Architecture Principles - API-First Principle (Draft) https://014gc.sharepoint.com/sites/66251/SitePages/BDM-Architecture-Principle.aspx BDM DAWG June 3, 2026 PROBLEM WORTH SOLVING As BDM progresses with multiple programs (e.g., OAS, EI, CPP) and parallel platform development (Standalone UA, Curam SPM, iSAMS, Knowledge Management, Contact Center as Service, Common Benefit Platform, Operational Data Platform, and etc.), there is a growing risk of fragmented integration patterns, tightly coupled solutions, and duplicated capabilities emerging across the ecosystem. Without a consistent integration approach that prioritizes loose coupling, independently evolving components may adopt ad-hoc patterns, ranging from point-to-point integrations to custom solutions. This fragmentation will reduce interoperability across programs, slow delivery, increases architectural complexity, and accumulate of technical debt over time. THE VISION BDM will adopt an API-first architecture principle to ensure that all systems and platforms evolve with a consistent, reusable, and interoperable integration model, preventing fragmentation and enabling decoupled channels, cross-program capabilities, readiness for external consumption and solid foundation for future AI-driven services. The BDM Architecture Principles are a set of guidelines and guardrails to guide the development of the BDM Programme. They expand on the guidelines of the IITB Architecture Principles, GC Digital Standards (https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/government-canada-digital-standards.html) and GC Architecture Framework (https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/policies-standards/government-canada-enterprise-architecture-framework.html), but are tailored to the specific environment and implementation goals of the BDM Programme. BDM currently maintains 14 principles, detailed on the BDM Architecture Principles (https://014gc.sharepoint.com/sites/66251/SitePages/BDM-Architecture-Principle.aspx) SharePoint page. The catalogue was recently refreshed in March 2026 (approved by DAB), with 4 new principles added, and revisions made to 3 existing principles.
